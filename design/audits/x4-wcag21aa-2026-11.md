# Portal accessibility and responsiveness audit — WCAG 2.1 AA, November 2026

Audit of the portal frontend against **WCAG 2.1 Level AA**, plus responsiveness at **1440 px
(desktop), 768 px (tablet) and 390 px (mobile)**. This is a findings report and nothing in it changes
product code. The prioritised fixes are in
[`x4-remediation-backlog-2026-11.md`](x4-remediation-backlog-2026-11.md).

## Headline

**37 WCAG 2.1 A/AA findings: 5 critical, 30 serious, 1 moderate, 1 minor.** Every one of the
**13 surfaces audited (of the 13 in scope)** has at least one.

- **26 come from axe-core** (4 critical, 22 serious). A finding here is one axe rule on one surface,
  so the same rule on two surfaces counts twice and 40 failing nodes of one rule on one surface count
  once. They cover 12 of the 13 surfaces. The session-expired surface is the only one axe passes, and
  the keyboard pass fails it.
- **11 come from the manual passes** (1 critical, 8 serious, 1 moderate, 1 minor): keyboard, token
  contrast and responsiveness. axe cannot see any of these.
- **13 best-practice results** (1 serious, 9 moderate, 3 minor) are reported apart and are **not**
  counted as WCAG failures.

The counts look bigger than the work behind them. **Most of the 26 axe findings come from four root
causes.** 12 of the 26 are `color-contrast`, and that rule's 136 failing elements come down mostly to two
theme decisions (§4.1). The **single largest defect is responsive, not semantic**: the shell does not
reflow. The 236 px sidebar never collapses, so the content area is 154 px wide on a 390 px phone and
84 px wide at the 320 px reflow width that WCAG 1.4.10 names (§6).

What is already in good shape, measured rather than assumed:

- A focus indicator draws on 128 of the 137 focus stops recorded in the keyboard pass, almost all of
  them the global ring (design rule C3). Of the 9 without one, 7 are the sortable table headers (K4).
  The other 2 are the Agentiko composer, which shows focus by tinting its 1 px border instead: weak,
  but visible.
- The shell has `main`, `nav`, `header` and two `aside` landmarks, and every page sets its own
  document title.
- Clickable table rows and composition rows can be reached and opened with the keyboard (C8).
- Every icon-only header control has an accessible name (C9).
- The Agentiko approval card is reachable one Shift+Tab from the composer, and both decisions work
  from the keyboard.
- No surface scrolls horizontally at any width. That is part of the problem, though: the content is
  squeezed instead (§6).

### 1.2 How the pages were rendered (offline, real product pages)

No cluster was used. Each page is the **real product page**, rendered from the portal's own page
charts by the **production build** of this repo:

1. **Frontend.** `vite build` of this commit, served by `vite preview`. This is the shipped bundle,
   with no dev-only overlays.
2. **Page definitions.** The product portal chart repository at commit `221bbbb`. Its `portal`,
   `dashboard-page`, `compositions-page`, `blueprints-page`, `audit-page`, `observability-page`,
   `settings-page`, `builders-page` and `marketplace-page` charts were rendered with
   `helm template` into 461 objects, all the widget CRs and RESTActions those charts ship.
3. **A mock snowplow, authn and agent endpoint**, intercepted in the browser (Playwright
   `route`):
   - `/call` answers each widget CR as snowplow would: `status.widgetData` is `spec.widgetData` with
     every `widgetDataTemplate` expression evaluated, and `status.resourcesRefs` comes from
     `spec.resourcesRefs` and `resourcesRefsTemplate`, with paths and `allowed: true`.
   - The templates are the charts' own jq, run with jq 1.7.1 against synthetic RESTAction outputs.
     Those outputs are 12 compositions in mixed states, 6 blueprints, events, 30 audit records,
     traces and access grants, all with invented, generic names. The jq output is therefore exactly
     the shape snowplow emits, because it is the same expression.
   - The sidebar's items are derived from the page roots' `krateo.io/nav-*` annotations, which is
     what the chart's `sidebar-nav` RESTAction does.
   - `/strategies` answers the four strategies. A failed sign-in answers authn's own 403
     `InvalidCredentials` Status.
   - The agent endpoint answers a JSON-RPC `message/stream` with an SSE turn: a task frame, a
     delegated tool call and its result, a cluster read and its result, streamed text, then an
     `input-required` pause carrying an `adk_request_confirmation` part. A decision answers with a
     completed turn.
4. **Session.** A fabricated `K_user` object with no real credential.

Two consequences matter when reading the findings:

- **Data is representative, not real.** Long names, empty states other than the ones staged, and
  counts in the thousands were not exercised.
- **Every finding is in code this repo ships**: frontend components, the theme, or antd as this repo
  configures it. Where a fix also needs a chart change, the backlog says so.

## 4. Automated findings in detail

### 4.1 Colour contrast (1.4.3) — three root causes behind 136 failing elements

`color-contrast` fails on 12 of 13 surfaces, counting each element once per surface. Grouping the
failing elements by foreground, background and component shows three causes. A fourth colour cause,
C3, is non-text contrast and appears in §5.3.

**C1. antd's alpha-derived secondary and placeholder text (serious).** `lightTheme` and `darkTheme`
in `ui/src/theme/tokens.ts` set `colorTextBase` but none of the derived text tokens:
`colorTextSecondary`, `colorTextDescription`, `colorTextTertiary`, `colorTextPlaceholder` and
`colorTextQuaternary`. antd derives them as alpha-blended text colours, and those blends fail on the
portal's grounds:

| Pair (measured by axe) | Ratio | Where it renders |
|---|---|---|
| light `#959595` on panel `#FFFFFF` | 2.99:1 | Statistic titles, Typography `secondary`, list descriptions, Steps descriptions, dialog descriptions |
| light `#919093` on page `#F7F6FB` | 2.95:1 | breadcrumb links, `Empty` descriptions, page notes |
| dark `#777679` on `#07050C` / `#827F88` on `#1C1626` | 4.48:1 / 4.47:1 | the same components in dark |
| light `#C4C4C4` on `#FFFFFF` | 1.74:1 | the header project select's resting text "All projects", on 14 of 15 signed-in scenarios |
| dark `#4D4A53` on `#120E1A` | 2.19:1 | the same, dark |

The palette already has passing tokens for exactly this role. `gray` (`#5E5873`, 6.7:1 on white) and
`faint` (`#6A6480`, 5.6:1) are pinned by T7. They are simply not wired into antd. Shots:
`dashboard-light-1440.png`, `audit-light-1440.png`.

**C2. The primary "wash" button label (serious).** `ui/index.css` restyles every
`type="primary"` button as a 14 % wash of `--primary-color` behind a `--primary-color` label. In
light mode the label measures:

- 4.18:1 at rest on a panel (`#9E00FF` on `#F1DBFF`)
- 3.93:1 on the page ground (`#EBD4FC`)
- 3.62:1 on hover and focus (`#EAC7FF`)

It renders at 12–14 px. Affected buttons include "Sign In", "Create composition", "Open app",
"Retry", "Publish", "Review" and the selected filter chip. Dark mode passes. T7's test pins primary
on white, which is not the pair that ships. Shots: `login-light-1440.png`,
`compositions-light-1440.png`.

**C4. Tag, chip and code colours (serious).** Individually small, together recurring:

| Element | Ratio |
|---|---|
| blue status tag, light (`#11B2E2` on `#DBF3FB`) | 2.14:1 |
| filter-chip count, light (`#BD55FE` on `#EBD4FC`) | 2.60:1 |
| filter-chip count, dark | 3.09:1 |
| Drift tag, dark (`#9350DB` on `#251837`) | 3.48:1 |
| green / gold filled tags on tinted cards, light | 3.58:1 / 3.01:1 |
| red tag, light | 4.41:1 |
| rail inline `code` and bold context, light (`--cyan-color` `#0E9488` on white) | 3.74:1 |
| page-builder YAML syntax highlighting (`#4286F4` on `#F7F6FB`) | 3.28:1 |
| settings audit-log literal colours (`#FF0000` "Denied" 3.99:1, `#008000` "Allowed" in dark 3.70:1, `#8C8C8C` 3.36:1) | — |

The last row is hex literals in the settings chart's `audit-log` table cells, which T1 forbids.

**C3 (manual, 1.4.11) appears in §5.3**, because axe does not evaluate non-text contrast.

### 4.2 `image-alt` (1.1.1, critical) — App shell

The sider footer (`SiderFooter` in `ui/src/components/Shell/Shell.tsx`) and the account-menu trigger
(`ui/src/components/UserMenu/UserMenu.tsx`) render `<Avatar src={avatarURL}>` with no `alt`. When the
identity has no avatar URL, antd still renders a bare `<img>`. The browser also logs "an empty string
was passed to the src attribute" on every page. 2 nodes on every signed-in page.

### 4.3 List and card semantics (1.3.1, 4.1.2, 1.1.1, serious)

All three rules land on the `Listy` and `Card` widgets:

- **`list`** (Dashboard, Composition detail, Observability). `ui/src/widgets/List/ListView.tsx` puts
  `role="button"` on the antd `List.Item` `<li>`, so the `<ul>` no longer contains list items. The
  same nodes raise `aria-allowed-role` (best-practice, 25 nodes on the dashboard alone).
- **`aria-progressbar-name`** (Dashboard ×14, Observability ×8, Composition detail ×1). The progress
  bars in `ListView` rows have no accessible name. A screen reader announces "progress bar, 83 %"
  with no subject.
- **`nested-interactive`** (Dashboard ×1, Blueprints ×6).
  - On the dashboard, a clickable `Card` (`role="button"`, `ui/src/widgets/Card/`) contains a
    focusable list.
  - On Blueprints, each blueprint tile is a `role="button"` card that also contains a "Create"
    button.
  - Assistive technology flattens a button's content, so the inner control is unreachable or
    announced twice.

### 4.4 `label` (4.1.2, critical) — Settings / access

The namespace picker on the access-detail page is the `Select` widget (CR `access-ns-select`, which
sets no `label`). `ui/src/widgets/Select/Select.tsx` uses `label` and `placeholder` only for the popup
header, never as the combobox's accessible name, so the input has none.

### 4.5 `scrollable-region-focusable` (2.1.1, serious) — 390 px

At narrow widths the YAML and code blocks overflow horizontally: the composition manifest `pre`, and
the page builder's 10 file previews (`#preview-file-*`). They cannot be scrolled from the keyboard,
because neither the block nor anything in it is focusable.

### 4.6 `aria-required-children` (1.3.1, critical) — 390 px

At narrow widths antd `Tabs` moves overflowing tabs into a "more" button that it places **inside**
`role="tablist"`. This is antd's markup, but it ships in the observability tabs and the page builder's
Files/Source tabs.

  theme). Counts per surface at 1440 px range from 4 (login) to 27 (page builder canvas).

## 7. Lighthouse (accessibility category, desktop preset)

**Not runnable here, so no scores are reported.** This was tried on four runs, with two drivers:

1. Lighthouse attached over CDP to the Playwright-owned Chromium that carried the mock routes.
2. Lighthouse launching its own Chrome against a real HTTP mock server and a copy of the build whose
   `config.json` points at it, with the session seeded into the Chrome profile.

Both were tried on `/login`, which needs no session, in light and dark. Every run ended the same way:
`Timed out waiting for page load. Checking if page is hung… Page appears to be hung, killing
JavaScript… unable to reliably load the URL`.

Two controls rule out a broken install and a frozen page:

- The same Lighthouse, in the same Chrome binary, scores a trivial static page 100.
- On the same build, a Playwright long-task probe at Lighthouse's desktop viewport (1350 × 940)
  found the main thread idle after load: one long task of at most 155 ms in 8 s on `/login` and on
  `/dashboard`, and a 100 ms timer firing within 141 ms.

So the failure is in how Lighthouse's navigation runner and this app interact. The page is not frozen.
The cause was not isolated.

Lighthouse's accessibility category is a weighted subset of the axe-core rules that were already run
on every surface (§4). Its absence loses the summary number, not any finding. Getting a Lighthouse run
working, against a deployed portal or with the cause found, is a backlog item.

## 8. Not covered, and why

- **Screen readers.** VoiceOver and NVDA were not used. Name, role and state come from axe and from
  Chromium's accessibility tree (Playwright `ariaSnapshot`), and live-region behaviour was inferred
  from markup.
- **Other browsers and real devices.** Only Chromium, desktop and emulated widths. No Safari or
  Firefox, and no touch.
- **Zoom and text spacing (1.4.4, 1.4.12), and content on hover (1.4.13).** Not measured. R1 makes
  the outcome at 200 % zoom on a 1280 px screen predictable, since it gives a 640 px viewport.
- **Live data and the live cluster.** The pages are the real chart output with synthetic data (§1.2).
  Very long names, very large lists, and the error and empty states that were not staged were not
  exercised. `helm template` does not run `lookup`, so any chart branch that depends on it rendered
  its default.
- **Builders other than the page builder.** The blueprint builder and controller builder composers
  were not opened. Within the page builder, drag-and-drop, the data-binding modals and the publish
  flow were not driven, though the source scan in K7 covers the binding modals' labels.
- **Surfaces outside this scope.** Marketplace, the notifications drawer, the account-menu popover,
  the composition edit drawer (YAML/form), the delete confirmation (BlastRadius), Agentiko
  conversation history and voice input.
- **The OIDC and GitHub redirect round trip** (`/auth`) and identity-provider pages. The
  social-login buttons were audited, the round trip was not.
- **Charts.** G2 charts draw on a canvas that axe cannot inspect. Text alternatives for charts (1.1.1)
  and chart colour contrast were not assessed, and the fixture left the throughput chart empty.

## 9. Reproducing this audit

The harness was about 900 lines in a scratch directory and is not part of this change:

- the mock backend and the chart-template evaluator
- the scenario runner
- the keyboard pass and its verifier
- the layout measurement
- the token-contrast check
- the Lighthouse driver

Landing it would mean a `ui/audit/` directory with its own Playwright config, kept out of the visual
job's `*.spec.ts` match, and a checkout of the portal charts as input. No new dependency is needed:
axe-core is already installed, and Lighthouse can run through `npx`. It is listed as the last
backlog item, because re-running this audit after the fixes is the only way to close it honestly.
